# The Devotion Journal — how it works

Two things were added to your website. This page explains both in plain
English, and exactly what you do each day.

---

## 1. The journal — for your people

**pastoradamsummers.com/journal**

Anyone can open that on their phone and add it to their home screen, where
it behaves like a normal app. It's a place to write down what they read in
Scripture each day: where they read, what it says, what they're taking away,
and a prayer. If they'd rather just have one blank box, there's a switch for
that.

Some things worth knowing when you tell people about it:

- **It works with no internet.** The whole King James Bible is inside the
  app, so when they type "John 15:1-8" the passage appears even with no
  signal.
- **What they write is private.** It is stored on their own phone. It is not
  sent to the church, it is not sent to you, and nobody at the website can
  read it. That's not a policy — the words are simply never uploaded.
- **It shows them their own faithfulness.** A streak, a total, and a little
  calendar of the days they've written. It never scolds anyone for missing.
  Someone coming back after three weeks away is welcomed, not measured.
- **Reading plans are optional.** Whole Bible in a year, Old and New
  Testament together, or the Gospels in ninety days. Off unless they choose
  one, and switching never loses anything they've written.
- **They can be reminded.** Optional, at a time they pick.

---

## 2. Sharing — how something reaches you

At the bottom of any entry there's a **Share this with Pastor Summers**
button. It opens a screen showing exactly what would be sent, which they can
edit first. It sends **that one entry only** — never their whole journal.

They choose how their name appears: first name, initials, or Anonymous. They
must tick a box saying they understand you'll read it first and that, if you
publish it, it will be public on the website.

They are **not** told whether you published it. That's deliberate — see below.

---

## 3. Your daily job

### The email

Once a day you'll get one email: **"3 new devotions awaiting review."** It
lists each one — who wrote it, the passage, and the first line or two — so
you can see what's there before opening anything. **On days when nothing is
waiting, no email is sent.**

### The review page

**pastoradamsummers.com/admin** — sign in with the devotions password.

You'll see the full text of every waiting devotion. You don't have to click
into them one at a time.

For each one you have three buttons:

- **Publish** — it goes live on the website straight away
- **Discard** — it disappears from your queue and nothing happens.
  **The person is never told.** No email, no notification, no reason needed.
  This is on purpose: it saves you from having to explain or soften anything.
- **Edit first** — fix a typo or tidy the wording, then publish

**If you'd rather use the keyboard,** it's much faster:

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| `J` / `K` | Move down / up the list |
| `A` | Publish the one you're on |
| `R` | Discard it |
| `E` | Edit it |
| `X` | Tick it, to publish or discard a batch together |

### Adding a note of your own

When you edit before publishing, there's a box called **"Your note"**. Put a
sentence or a verse of your own in there and it appears under their words on
the website, clearly set apart as yours. It's a good way to turn someone's
reflection into a teaching moment without changing what they wrote.

### Things marked with a flag

Anything with an orange **⚑** was picked up by the automatic check — bad
language, ALL CAPS, or something very similar to a devotion already sent.
Those are sorted to the top so you see them first. A flag isn't a judgement,
just a "look at this one properly."

Anything containing a web link or an email address is rejected before it ever
reaches you. That's almost always spam.

## 4. Changing the website itself

Different page, same sign-in: **pastoradamsummers.com/keystatic**. That is
where you change the site itself rather than review what others have sent —
sermons, blog posts, family music, the photos, the About page, your email
address and the verses in the header. Anything you save there appears on the
website about a minute later.

To hop between the two, use the **Devotions | Site editor** switch at the
top of either page — same switch, same place, one tap, and you never sign
in twice.| Site editor** switch. It sits
at the top of the review page and floats in the bottom-right corner of the
editor — same switch, same place, one tap, and you never sign in twice.

A simple way to keep them straight: **the site editor is for your words; the
review page is for everyone else's.**

## 5. Taking something down

**pastoradamsummers.com/admin/published** lists everything currently public.
Each one has a **Take down** button. One click and it's off the website
immediately.

Every published devotion also has a small "Request removal" link at the
bottom, so if someone changes their mind they can email you and you can pull
it in seconds.

---

## 6. What you can and can't see

You can see, on your review page, a few plain numbers: how many people wrote
in their journal this week, how many entries there were, and how many people
have the app.

**You cannot see what anybody wrote.** Those numbers are counts and nothing
else — no names, no email addresses, no text. The only words you will ever
read are the ones somebody deliberately sent you. If anyone asks whether the
pastor can read their journal, the answer is a flat no.

## If something looks wrong

Tell Steven. Nothing you do on the review page can break the website, and
